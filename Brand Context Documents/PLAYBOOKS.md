# Sales Playbook

## Our Approach

We are highly selective about early customers. We prioritize:
1. **1st degree network contacts** — People we know directly
2. **Warm introductions** — Referrals from trusted connections
3. **Inbound interest** — People who reach out to us
4. **Cold email/LinkedIn replies** — Only if they respond positively

We do NOT do mass outreach. Every conversation should feel personal because it is.

**Goal:** Initial connection → Build relationship → In-person meeting → Product demo

- [ ] No helpdesk software

## Process: 1st Degree Network Outreach

### Step 1: Identify & Research

Before reaching out:
- Confirm they match our ICP
- Check what they're working on currently
- Look for recent news, launches, or challenges
- Identify the personal connection angle

### Step 2: Personal Reach Out

**Channel:** WhatsApp, personal email, or LinkedIn DM — whichever feels most natural for that relationship.

**Tone:** Casual, personal. This is not a sales pitch. You're reconnecting and sharing something relevant.

**Template approach:**
```
Hey [Name],

[Personal connection / how you know them]

[Reason for reaching out — something relevant to them]

[Brief mention of what we're building — only if it connects to their situation]

Would love to catch up — [coffee/call/lunch]?
```

**Example:**
> Hey Priya,
>
> Been a while since the WebEngage days! Saw you're killing it at Purple — congrats on the expansion.
>
> We're building something in the e-commerce support space and honestly, you'd be the perfect person to get feedback from. Not a sales thing — just want to learn from someone who actually runs support at scale.
>
> Free for a coffee next week? I'm around Indiranagar most days.

### Step 3: In-Person Meeting

**Goal:** Build relationship, understand their world, assess if there's a fit.

**This is NOT a demo.** Don't bring slides. Don't pitch.

**Conversation flow:**
1. Catch up personally (5-10 min)
2. Ask about their business and challenges (15-20 min)
3. Share what you're building — only if relevant to their challenges (5-10 min)
4. If there's interest, propose a demo as next step

**Questions to explore:**
- How's the support team structured?
- What's eating up most of their time?
- Have they tried automation before? What worked/didn't?
- What would "solved" look like for them?

### Step 4: Product Demo (If Invited)

**Only do a demo if:**
- They've expressed genuine interest
- You've had an in-person/video conversation first
- They understand what we do at a high level

**Demo structure (30 min):**

| Time | Focus |
|------|-------|
| 0-5 min | Confirm their context and what they want to see |
| 5-20 min | Show product tailored to their specific use case |
| 20-25 min | Q&A |
| 25-30 min | Discuss next steps (pricing conversation, trial, etc.) |

### Step 5: Pricing Conversation

**Do not discuss pricing in the demo.**

If they ask about pricing:
> "Pricing depends on volume and setup. Let's schedule a quick call to go through the specifics — I want to make sure I give you accurate numbers for your situation."

→ **Schedule a separate pricing conversation** (see POLICIES.md)

## Process: Warm Introductions

### Requesting an Intro

When asking a mutual connection for an introduction:

**Template:**
```
Hey [Connector],

Quick favor — I'd love an intro to [Target Name] at [Company].

We're building AI support automation for e-commerce, and [reason they'd be relevant — e.g., "they're scaling fast and probably drowning in tickets"].

Totally fine if it's not the right time or you'd rather not — no pressure.

Here's a forwardable blurb if helpful:
---
[2-3 sentences about Resolvely and why you'd like to connect]
---

Thanks!
```

### After the Intro

1. Reply promptly (within 24 hours)
2. Thank the connector (move them to BCC after first reply)
3. Keep it light — suggest a call or coffee, don't pitch
4. Follow the same process as 1st degree outreach

## Process: Inbound Inquiries

When someone reaches out to us:

### Step 1: Respond Quickly (Within 4 hours)

**Template:** `TEMPLATES/responses/new_inquiry_reply.md`

### Step 2: Qualify

Understand:
- How did they hear about us?
- What's their ticket volume?
- What helpdesk/platform are they on?
- What's driving them to look at this now?

### Step 3: Route Appropriately

| Situation | Action |
|-----------|--------|
| Strong fit, decision maker | Schedule call/meeting |
| Strong fit, not decision maker | Ask to involve decision maker |
| Unclear fit | Ask qualifying questions |
| Not a fit | Send polite decline (TEMPLATES/responses/not_a_fit.md) |

## Process: LinkedIn Engagement

### Building Relationships (Before Outreach)

1. Connect with relevant people in e-commerce/D2C space
2. Engage genuinely with their content (comment, share)
3. Build familiarity over 2-4 weeks
4. Then reach out with a personal message

### LinkedIn DM (After Engagement)

**Only message people who:**
- Have accepted your connection
- You've engaged with their content
- Are clearly in your ICP

**Template:**
```
Hey [Name],

Been enjoying your posts about [topic]. The one about [specific post] really resonated.

We're building AI support automation for e-commerce brands — seems like it might be relevant given [their context].

Would you be open to a quick call? Happy to share what we're seeing in the space.
```

## Resume Review Process

**Check for must-haves:**

- [ ] Has worked with Python OR JavaScript/TypeScript

**Pull out and note:**
- Relevant skills and technologies
- Evidence of impact (look for numbers, outcomes)

| Verdict | Criteria | Action |

| **No** | Missing critical requirements or red flags | Send rejection within 24 hours |

## Interview Questions Bank

### Phone Screen (All Roles)

- Tell me about yourself (2 min version)
- What do you know about Resolvely?
- Why are you looking to move?
- What are you looking for in your next role?
- What are your salary expectations?
- When can you start?

### Engineering Technical Round

- Walk me through a project you're proud of. What was hard?
- [Coding] Solve [problem] — talk through your approach
- How would you design a system to [relevant scenario]?
- Tell me about a time you debugged a tough production issue
- How do you stay updated on new technologies?

### Customer Success Case Study

- "A customer says our AI gave a wrong response to their customer. They're upset. How do you handle it?"
- "A customer hasn't logged in for 3 weeks. What do you do?"
- "A customer wants a feature we don't have. How do you respond?"

### Culture Fit (All Roles)

- Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. What happened?
- What's a mistake you made at work? What did you learn?
- How do you handle ambiguity?
- What does good feedback look like to you?

# Customer Support Playbook

## Ticket Priority

| Priority | Definition | Response Time | Examples |
|----------|------------|---------------|----------|
| **P1 - Urgent** | Customer blocked, revenue at risk | Within 2 hours | System down, billing error, data loss |
| **P2 - High** | Significant issue, customer frustrated | Within 4 hours | Integration broken, wrong responses going out |
| **P3 - Normal** | Standard question or issue | Within 24 hours | How-to questions, feature requests |
| **P4 - Low** | Nice to know, not urgent | Within 48 hours | General feedback, suggestions |

---

## Escalation Triggers

**Escalate immediately if:**
- Customer threatens to cancel
- Customer mentions competitor
- Same issue reported 3+ times
- Customer asks for refund
- Data integrity issue
- Security concern
- System-wide outage
- Legal threat or mention of lawyer
- Press inquiry

---

## Common Scenarios

### Customer: "Your AI gave a wrong answer"

1. Apologize for the inconvenience
2. Ask for specific ticket ID / example
3. Review what happened
4. Explain and fix (retrain AI if needed)
5. Follow up to confirm resolution

### Customer: "We want to cancel"

1. Understand why (don't be defensive)
2. See if issue is fixable
3. If not fixable, process cancellation gracefully
4. Log detailed reason for churn analysis
5. Escalate to leadership

### Customer: "We need a feature you don't have"

1. Thank them for the feedback
2. Understand the use case (why do they need it?)
3. Check if there's a workaround
4. Log in feature request tracker
5. Don't promise timeline unless confirmed with product team
